The World of Hyatt Credit Card is the best $95 hotel card for anyone who spends even one night a year at a Hyatt, and the current offer of 4 free nights at Category 1–4 hotels is worth $5,000 of spend if that $5,000 is spending you were going to do anyway and you can actually book four Hyatt nights within 12 months of the certificates posting. If either of those conditions fails, the offer is weaker than it looks, and a flexible card like the Chase Sapphire Preferred is the smarter use of the same $5,000.

What keeps the card in a wallet after year one is simple: a Category 1–4 free night every cardmember anniversary, a second one for $15,000 of calendar-year spend, automatic Discoverist status and a steady drip of tier-qualifying night credits. The weakness is just as simple: outside of Hyatt, the earning is ordinary.

Welcome Offer: 4 Free Nights After $5,000

Chase's current offer, labeled limited-time on the issuer page with no end date given, is 4 Free Night Awards after $5,000 in purchases within the first 3 months of account opening. Each award is good for one night in a standard room at a Category 1–4 Hyatt hotel or resort and expires 12 months from issuance. The fine print matters:

  • The awards are not valid at Miraval, Hyatt Zilara or Hyatt Ziva resorts, or for packages.
  • Chase says to allow 6–8 weeks after qualifying for the awards to post.
  • If the account is not open for at least 6 months, Hyatt and Chase can take the awards back.
  • The offer is not available to current cardmembers or anyone who received a new cardmember bonus on this card in the past 24 months.

What are the four certificates worth?

There is no single dollar answer, because Hyatt prices award nights by category and date. Category 1–4 hotels price between 3,000 and 25,000 points per night, according to The Points Guy's review updated November 11, 2026, and Frequent Miler (November 5, 2026) notes that Category 4 tops out at 25,000 points at peak pricing. That means four certificates can replace up to 100,000 points of peak Category 4 award nights, or far less if they end up at Category 1 and 2 hotels. Frequent Miler's own estimate is about $240 per certificate, or $960 for the set.

The comparison that matters: the same $5,000 in 3 months on the Chase Sapphire Preferred earns its 75,000-point bonus, which converts to 56,250 Hyatt points at the current 4:3 transfer ratio. Those points never expire on a 12-month clock and can go to any Hyatt category. The four certificates win on peak Category 4 nights; the Sapphire points win on flexibility.

Chase 5/24 Rule

Frequent Miler (November 5, 2026) reports that the World of Hyatt Credit Card is subject to Chase's 5/24 rule. Chase does not publish the rule on the card page. If you have opened 5 or more cards across all issuers in the past 24 months, read how the Chase 5/24 rule works before applying.

The 2x categories are a reasonable set for a $95 card, but 1x on everything else is the card's worst feature. Anyone putting serious spend toward the $15,000 free night or extra night credits is giving up the better everyday earning on other Chase cards to do it.

Annual Free Nights

Every year after the cardmember anniversary, the primary cardmember receives a Category 1–4 Free Night Award, valid for 12 months. Spend $15,000 in a calendar year and a second Category 1–4 Free Night Award follows, once per calendar year. On both of these ongoing awards, Chase's terms say resort, destination and facility fees are waived on the redeemed night (Hyatt Residence Club excluded). The welcome offer terms carry no such waiver language, so do not assume it applies to those four nights.

One certificate at a hotel that would otherwise cost more than $95 in cash covers the fee. That is the whole case for keeping the card, and for most Hyatt guests it is an easy one.

Who Should Get the World of Hyatt Card?

Get it if you:

  • Stay at Hyatt at least once a year and will use the anniversary night
  • Can put $5,000 of normal spending on the card in 3 months without stretching
  • Already have trips that can absorb four Category 1–4 nights within a year
  • Want to add Hyatt status credits from spending

Skip it if you:

  • Rarely stay at Hyatt; the certificates will expire unused
  • Want one card for everyday spending; the 1x base rate is weak
  • Haven't yet opened a Sapphire Preferred; its bonus transfers to Hyatt at 4:3 and works for flights too
  • Are at or over Chase 5/24

FAQ

Is the World of Hyatt Credit Card worth it in 2026?

Yes, for anyone who stays at Hyatt yearly. The anniversary Category 1–4 night alone can cover the $95 fee, and status and night credits come on top.

Do the 4 free nights expire?

Yes. Each welcome Free Night Award is valid for 12 months from issuance, and the stay's checkout must be before the expiration date.

Can I use the certificates at any Hyatt?

No. They work only at Category 1–4 hotels and resorts in standard rooms, and not at Miraval, Hyatt Zilara or Hyatt Ziva.

Can Chase Ultimate Rewards points move to Hyatt?

Yes. The Sapphire Preferred (since June 15, 2026) and Ink Business Preferred (since October 1, 2026) transfer to Hyatt at 4:3. World of Hyatt card points cannot move the other way into Ultimate Rewards.

Does the card charge foreign transaction fees?

No.

The Bottom Line

The World of Hyatt Credit Card is a keeper card disguised as a sign-up bonus. The 4-free-night offer is a strong reason to open it now, but only for someone who will book those nights within a year. The card earns its spot in a wallet through the anniversary night, and that part is worth having for any Hyatt guest at $95.
