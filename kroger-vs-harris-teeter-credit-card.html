Kroger vs. Harris Teeter Rewards Credit Card 2026: Which Grocery Card Is Right for You?

Two nearly identical cards relaunched in fall 2026 — same issuer (First Bank & Trust, powered by Imprint), same rewards structure, same $0 annual fee. The real comparison isn't between the cards. It's about which stores are in your area.

Here's the honest truth about this comparison: the Kroger Rewards World Elite Mastercard and the Harris Teeter Rewards World Elite Mastercard are essentially the same card with different branding. Both were relaunched in fall 2026 (Kroger on September 30, Harris Teeter on October 30) and are now issued by First Bank & Trust with Imprint running the program, both are World Elite Mastercards accepted everywhere, both charge no annual fee, and both run on an identical 5-4-3-2-1 points structure. The only material difference is which chain's website, app, stores and fuel pumps earn the bonus rates.

If you live in a market served by Kroger-branded stores (Kroger, Ralphs, King Soopers, Smith's, etc.), get the Kroger card. If you live in the Mid-Atlantic or Southeast where Harris Teeter dominates, get the Harris Teeter card. Both grocery chains are owned by The Kroger Company, so you're dealing with the same parent company either way.

The Old Mobile Wallet Trick Is Gone

The U.S. Bank versions of these cards paid 5% on mobile wallet purchases anywhere. The 2026 relaunched cards do not. The top rates now apply only inside your chain — up to 5X on pickup and delivery orders through the Kroger or Harris Teeter website or app, 4X at the chain's fuel pumps and up to 3X in store — and points redeem for dollars off groceries and fuel discounts. Outside the chain you earn 2X on dining, utilities and travel and 1X on everything else.

The in-store 2% rate applies to your specific chain's locations — not just the branded store, but all stores under the Kroger Family of Companies umbrella. Here's what counts for each card:

An important note: Kroger card holders also earn 2% at Harris Teeter, because Harris Teeter is part of the Kroger Family of Companies. So in areas where both Kroger and Harris Teeter stores exist, the Kroger card is actually slightly more versatile — it covers more store formats at the 2% rate.

Fuel Points — A Hidden Benefit for Both Cards

Both cards earn 4X points at your chain's own fuel pumps, and points double as fuel savings: every 100 points knocks 10¢ off per gallon at participating Kroger Family or Harris Teeter fuel centers, up to $1 per gallon on up to 35 gallons per fill-up. For families who fill up regularly and shop at either chain, this benefit can be worth $100–$200+ per year in fuel savings. (The old U.S. Bank cards' bonus fuel points and 55¢/gallon first-year discount ended with the 2026 relaunch.)

No Earning Caps — But Watch the Redemption Rules

Unlike the old U.S. Bank cards' $3,000 mobile-wallet cap, the relaunched cards have no spending caps on bonus points. The trade-off is how you redeem: points go toward dollars off groceries and fuel discounts at your chain, and the $100 welcome reward is redeemable only on eligible grocery purchases (not fuel). If you want cash you can spend anywhere, a flat-rate cash back card is more flexible.

Is Either Card Worth Getting at All?

For regular Kroger or Harris Teeter shoppers: yes. The $100 welcome reward (after $750 in spending outside the chain within 90 days), the free Boost Essential/HT Plus membership ($69 value) and the $10 annual birthday reward add up to $179 in first-year perks before you earn a single point. Add up to 5X on pickup and delivery orders and 4X at the pump, and loyal shoppers come out well ahead of a general-purpose no-fee card. After the first year, the ongoing value depends on how much you spend with your chain — 2X on dining and 1X elsewhere, redeemable only at the grocery store, trail the best general cards outside the chain.

How These Cards Compare to General Grocery Cards

The honest comparison you should also make is against general cash back cards that earn on groceries everywhere:

  • Amex Blue Cash Preferred ($95/yr) — 6% on U.S. supermarkets up to $6,000/year. Higher rate than both Kroger/HT cards, but Kroger and Harris Teeter are coded as superstores so they may not qualify. Confirm with Amex before applying.
  • Amex Gold Card ($325/yr) — 4x on U.S. supermarkets (similar superstore coding caveat). Best for high grocery spenders who also dine out.
  • Citi Double Cash ($0/yr) — 2% on everything including groceries. No cap, no coding issues. Simpler but lower ceiling.

The Kroger/HT cards have an edge on your chain's own pickup and delivery orders (up to 5X) and fuel (4X). In store, up to 3X is competitive — but points are only redeemable at your chain, and outside it 1X–2X trails most general-purpose options.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I have both the Kroger and Harris Teeter cards?

Technically yes — they're separate products. But given that they share the same rewards structure and issuer (First Bank & Trust via Imprint), and the Kroger card already covers Harris Teeter at its in-store bonus rate, there's no practical reason to hold both. The Kroger card is the more versatile choice if you have access to both store formats.

Is there still a 5% mobile wallet bonus?

No — the 5% mobile wallet rate belonged to the old U.S. Bank cards and ended with the 2026 relaunch. The top rate now applies to pickup and delivery orders through your chain's website or app (up to 5X), with 4X at your chain's fuel pumps, up to 3X in store, 2X on dining, utilities and travel, and 1X on everything else.

What happens to the free Boost/HT Plus membership if I close the card?

You'll be charged for the remaining membership period if you don't cancel before closing the card. Cancel your Boost or HT Plus membership first, then close the card if you wish. The membership currently costs $69/year — worth keeping in mind before canceling.
