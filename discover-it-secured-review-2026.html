The Discover it® Secured Cash Back Credit Card is still the best secured credit card for most people building or rebuilding credit. Capital One relaunched it on October 5, 2026 with a new name and better terms: 5% cash back on rotating quarterly categories (up to $1,500 in purchases a quarter when you activate), 1% on everything else, a required deposit of just $49, $99 or $200, and the same unlimited first-year Cashback Match. There's no annual fee and no credit score required to apply. The one step backward: Discover no longer promises an account review at seven months.

What Changed in the October 2026 Relaunch

Applications for the old Discover it Secured were paused in June 2026 while Discover's cards moved over to Capital One, which bought Discover in 2025. The card that came back is a different product in several ways:

How the Deposit and Credit Line Work

A secured card needs a refundable security deposit. With the Discover it Secured Cash Back, Discover asks for $49, $99 or $200 depending on your credit profile, and every approved applicant gets a credit line of at least $200. That's the biggest improvement in the relaunch: someone asked for a $49 deposit gets four times as much credit line as they put down.

You have 35 days after approval to fund the deposit, and you can pay it in installments of at least $20 by electronic transfer. If you deposit more than the required amount before the account opens, your starting line goes up, to a maximum of $1,000 to $3,000 that Discover assigns when you're approved. Discover may also raise your line based on your payment history without asking for more deposit.

The card works like any other credit card everywhere Discover is accepted, and it reports to all three major credit bureaus. You get your deposit back as a statement credit when you graduate to an unsecured card, or when you close the account with the balance paid in full.

The 5% categories are what set this card apart. Most secured cards earn nothing, and the few that do usually pay a flat 1% or 1.5%. Maxing out a quarter's $1,500 at 5% earns $75 in cash back, and the Cashback Match doubles that in your first year. Activation isn't retroactive, so purchases you make before activating a quarter earn just 1%. Check Discover's cashback calendar each quarter and activate as soon as the window opens.

On a $200 starting line, you won't be able to put $1,500 a quarter on the card while keeping your balance low, and you shouldn't try. Use it for the category spending you already do, and pay the balance off before the statement closes.

Keep Utilization Low on a Small Line

Credit utilization, your balance as a share of your limit, is one of the biggest factors in your credit score. On a $200 line, a $150 balance is 75% utilization, which hurts. Pay the card down before the statement date so the balance reported to the bureaus stays low. If you can afford it, add to your deposit before the account opens for a bigger line: on a $1,000 line, keeping the balance under $300 keeps utilization at 30% or below.

Graduating to an Unsecured Card

Discover reviews secured accounts periodically and, after a positive track record of on-time payments, can return your deposit as a statement credit, make the account unsecured and upgrade you to the Discover it Cash Back. Your account history carries over. What's gone is the old promise of automatic reviews starting at seven months. Discover now gives no fixed timeline, so the speed of graduating depends on your payment history and overall credit.

APR and Fees

The card has no annual fee, no foreign transaction fee and no penalty APR. Your first late payment has no fee; after that, late fees are up to $41. The regular APR is 29.24% variable for purchases, balance transfers and cash advances, with no intro APR offer. That's high, which makes the usual rule even more important on a credit-building card: pay in full every month. You also get a free FICO Score 8, based on TransUnion data, through Capital One's CreditWise.

Bottom Line

The Discover it Secured Cash Back is still the best secured credit card for almost everyone. The relaunch made it cheaper to open, with deposits from $49 and a $200 minimum line, and more rewarding, with the same 5% categories as Discover's unsecured cash back card plus the first-year Cashback Match. The loss of the guaranteed 7-month review is a real downgrade, and the 29.24% APR means it only works if you pay in full. Activate the categories, keep your balance low, pay on time, and this card should move you to an unsecured card.
