The Amazon Prime Visa is a Chase-issued card built for one kind of shopper: someone who already pays for Prime and buys a lot from Amazon.com or Whole Foods Market. For that person it is hard to beat. It earns unlimited 5% back at Amazon.com, Audible.com, Whole Foods Market and on Chase Travel purchases, 2% at gas stations, restaurants and on local transit and commuting, and 1% everywhere else, with no annual fee and no foreign transaction fees.

Right now Chase is also running a limited-time welcome offer: Prime members get a $200 Amazon gift card loaded into their Amazon account on approval, up from the $150 Chase shows as the regular amount. This review covers how the rewards work, what the Prime requirement really means, and who should pick a different card.

Limited-Time Offer: $200 Amazon Gift Card on Approval

Chase labels this a limited-time offer and shows $200 in place of the usual $150. No end date is posted, so the amount can change without notice. Check the offer on Chase's page before you apply.

Welcome Offer: $200 Amazon Gift Card (Limited Time)

Eligible Prime members get a $200 Amazon gift card instantly loaded into their Amazon account's Gift Card Balance on approval. Unlike most welcome bonuses, there is no spending requirement: the gift card is awarded at the time of card approval, and only to new cardmembers. That makes it one of the easiest bonuses to earn of any no-annual-fee card.

The trade-off is flexibility. The bonus is an Amazon gift card, not cash back, so it is only worth face value if you would spend that money at Amazon anyway. For a Prime household, that is usually a safe bet. Chase shows a smaller gift card offer for applicants without a Prime membership.

The 5% Amazon.com category is broader than many people expect. Chase's terms include digital downloads, Amazon Fresh orders, Amazon Prime subscriptions and items sold by third-party merchants through Amazon's marketplace. The Whole Foods 5% covers physical Whole Foods Market locations in the United States.

Rewards are tracked as points: each $1 in rewards equals 100 points, so 5% back works out to 5 points per dollar. Points don't expire as long as your account stays open, and you can redeem them for cash, travel, or eligible purchases at Amazon.com, as soon as the next day.

The Prime Membership Requirement

The headline 5% rate, and the $200 welcome gift card, both depend on having an eligible Prime membership. Without Prime, the Amazon and Whole Foods rate drops to 3%. That matters because Prime itself is a paid subscription. If you would not otherwise pay for Prime, the card's value case gets much weaker, and a flat-rate card is often the better choice.

If you already have Prime for shipping and streaming, the math is simple: the card adds no annual fee on top, and every Amazon and Whole Foods dollar earns 5%.

The example shows where the card wins and where it doesn't. The $420 earned at Amazon and Whole Foods does most of the work. The $1,000 a month of general spending earns only 1%, which is half of what a 2% flat-rate card would pay. Many cardholders pair the Prime Visa with a flat-rate card for everything outside Amazon, Whole Foods and the 2% categories.

Fees and APR

The Prime Visa has no annual fee and no foreign transaction fees, so it also works for purchases abroad. The variable purchase APR is 18.99%–27.74%. Like any rewards card, it only makes sense if you pay the balance in full; interest at that rate erases 5% back quickly.

Other Benefits

  • Travel and purchase protection: Chase lists travel and purchase protection benefits for the card. Check the Guide to Benefits for the exact coverages and limits before you rely on them.
  • Contactless payments: Tap to pay anywhere contactless is accepted.
  • Flexible redemptions: Cash, travel, or Amazon.com purchases, with points that don't expire while your account is open.

Who Should Get the Amazon Prime Visa?

Get it if you:

  • Already pay for Prime and shop regularly at Amazon.com or Whole Foods Market
  • Want an easy welcome offer with no minimum spend
  • Want a no-annual-fee card that also works abroad without foreign transaction fees

Skip it if you:

  • Don't have Prime and don't plan to get it: the rate drops to 3% and the welcome offer is smaller
  • Put most of your spending outside Amazon, Whole Foods and dining/gas: a 2% flat-rate card will likely earn more
  • Plan to carry a balance from month to month

The Bottom Line

For Prime members, the Amazon Prime Visa is close to a no-brainer: no annual fee, 5% back on Amazon and Whole Foods spending, useful 2% categories, no foreign transaction fees, and a limited-time $200 gift card that arrives the moment you're approved. Its weakness is the 1% rate on everything else, so the smartest setup is to use it for Amazon, Whole Foods, gas, dining and transit, and pair it with a flat-rate card for the rest.
