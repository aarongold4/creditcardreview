Best Credit Card Sign-Up Bonuses of 2026: Up to $1,500+ in Your First Year

A generous sign-up bonus is the fastest way to earn hundreds — or thousands — of dollars in rewards. We compared every major welcome offer available in April 2026 by value, spend requirement, and ongoing rewards.

⏱ 11 min read

A credit card sign-up bonus — also called a welcome offer — is a one-time reward you earn by spending a set amount within the first few months of opening a card. The best bonuses in 2026 are worth $750 to over $1,500 in travel, cash, or points — often enough to cover several round-trip flights or multiple free hotel nights.

But not all bonuses are created equal. A 175,000-point offer that requires $12,000 in spending may be less valuable than a 75,000-point offer that requires only $4,000. We evaluated every major offer by three factors: total value, spend requirement, and the card's ongoing rewards after the bonus is earned.

How to Maximize Any Sign-Up Bonus

Time your application strategically

Apply right before a large planned expense — a vacation, home repair, new appliance, or annual insurance premium. Using the card for purchases you'd make anyway is the cleanest way to hit the spending threshold without changing your habits.

Put all regular spending on the new card

For the first 3 months, route every dollar you'd normally spend through the new card — groceries, gas, utilities, subscriptions, dining. Most people are surprised how quickly $4,000–$5,000 accumulates from normal monthly spending.

Pay bills with the card where possible

Many utilities, insurance providers, and subscription services accept credit cards. Temporarily routing recurring bills through your new card is a legitimate way to boost spending toward the threshold.

Don't overspend to hit the threshold

A $200 bonus on a $500 requirement is free money. A $750 bonus that requires $1,000 in manufactured spending on things you didn't need is a bad deal. Only pursue bonuses you can hit through genuine spending.

Important: One Bonus Per Card, Lifetime Rules

Most issuers limit sign-up bonuses to once per card per lifetime — or in Chase's case, once per card with a proprietary screening system. If you've held a card before and earned its bonus, you may not qualify again. Chase also enforces a 5/24 rule — if you've opened 5+ new cards from any issuer in the past 24 months, Chase will typically deny your application.

Cash Back Bonuses vs. Points Bonuses — Which Is Better?

Cash back bonuses — like the $100 after $500 spend on the Wells Fargo Active Cash — are simple and guaranteed. $100 is worth exactly $100.

Points and miles bonuses are more complex but almost always more valuable when redeemed for travel. 75,000 Chase points are worth $750 when redeemed through Chase Travel at 1 cent per point (more with Points Boost). Transfer those same points to Hyatt (56,250 Hyatt points at the 4:3 ratio) and book a Category 4 hotel at $300/night for 15,000 points per night — and your 75,000 points are suddenly worth about $1,125 in hotel stays.

The rule of thumb: if you travel at least once a year and are willing to spend 30 minutes learning how to transfer points, points bonuses consistently outperform cash back bonuses of similar size.

The Bottom Line

For most people who want the best first-year value at a reasonable fee, the Chase Sapphire Preferred is the best sign-up bonus available. Its 75,000-point bonus, $95 annual fee, and strong ongoing rewards make it the most complete package on this list.

If you travel frequently and can justify a higher fee, the Chase Sapphire Reserve's 100,000-point bonus is one of the most valuable offers from Chase — and combined with the $300 travel credit, the effective net cost in year one is essentially zero.

For business owners, nothing beats the Ink Business Preferred's 100,000-point bonus at a $95 annual fee — the highest bonus-to-fee ratio of any card on the market right now.
