The Blue Cash Preferred is the best cash back card for grocery shoppers. 6% at U.S. supermarkets is the highest grocery rate available on any mainstream credit card. Combined with 6% on streaming services, 3% on gas and transit, and the $84–$120/year Disney Bundle credit, the BCP typically returns several hundred dollars annually for households with meaningful grocery and commuting costs. The $95 annual fee (waived year one) pays for itself at just $264/month in grocery spending.

$10/Month Disney Bundle Credit

Use your Blue Cash Preferred on an eligible auto-renewing Disney+, Hulu, or ESPN+ (Disney Bundle) subscription each month and receive a $10 statement credit — $120/year potential value. Since the Disney Bundle costs $14–$18/month, you're effectively paying $4–$8/month for it. This single benefit covers nearly the entire $95 annual fee for Disney Bundle subscribers.

Break-Even: When BCP Beats the No-Fee Blue Cash Everyday

BCP charges $95/yr while BCE is free. BCP earns 6% vs. BCE's 3% on groceries — a 3% difference. Break-even on grocery spending alone: $95 ÷ 0.03 = $3,167/yr (~$264/month). For most U.S. families spending $350–$600/month on groceries, BCP earns $100–$200+ more per year than BCE after the fee.

Supermarket Exclusions — Important

The 6% rate applies to U.S. supermarkets only. These popular stores earn just 1%:

  • Walmart and Walmart Neighborhood Markets
  • Target and Super Target
  • Costco, Sam's Club, BJ's Wholesale Club
  • Meal kit delivery services (HelloFresh, Blue Apron, etc.)

Traditional supermarkets qualify: Kroger, Safeway, Albertsons, Publix, HEB, Wegmans, Trader Joe's, Whole Foods, Sprouts, ShopRite, Stop & Shop, and most regional grocery chains.

Bottom Line

The Blue Cash Preferred is the right card for families who shop at traditional supermarkets and subscribe to streaming services. The 6% grocery rate is unmatched, the Disney Bundle credit covers most of the annual fee, and the 3% on transit makes it a strong everyday card for commuters. The grocery cap and Walmart/Costco exclusions are the meaningful limitations — but for households spending $250–$500/month at traditional supermarkets, the BCP returns significantly more cash back than any comparable card at the same price tier.
