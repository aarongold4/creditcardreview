The Chase Freedom Flex is one of the most versatile no-annual-fee cards available and the best rotating category card on the market. Its 5% on rotating quarterly categories, paired with permanent 3% on dining and drugstores, can earn $300+ per year in bonus cash back — for free. Add no foreign transaction fees and the ability to stack rewards with a Chase Sapphire card to unlock airline transfer partners, and you have a card that punches well above its $0 price point.

Q4 2026 (active now through December 31) is a standout quarter: grocery stores (excluding Walmart and Target), dining and American Red Cross at 5%. Activate by December 14, 2026 — activations apply retroactively to October 1 so you don't lose any bonus earnings.

The Sapphire Stacking Strategy

The Freedom Flex earns Chase Ultimate Rewards points, not just cash. If you also hold a Chase Sapphire Preferred ($95/yr) or Sapphire Reserve ($795/yr), you can combine all points from both cards and transfer to Chase's 14 airline and hotel partners — United, Southwest, Air France/KLM, British Airways, World of Hyatt, Marriott Bonvoy, and more.

When you do this, your 5% Freedom Flex categories become 5 transferable Ultimate Rewards points per dollar. Per TPG's April 2026 valuations at 2.05¢ per point, that's a 10.25% effective return on rotating category spending — from a no-annual-fee card. This "Chase Trifecta" strategy (Freedom Flex + Freedom Unlimited + Sapphire Preferred) is the most popular multi-card setup among points enthusiasts.

Cell Phone Protection Ended in September 2026

Chase removed the Freedom Flex's cell phone protection in September 2026 (it previously paid up to $800 per claim with a $50 deductible and a $1,000 yearly cap; claims had to be filed by September 19, 2026). Cards such as the Wells Fargo Active Cash still offer cell phone protection.

The right answer for most people: hold both. Use Freedom Flex in its 5% quarters for that category (max $75/quarter = $300/year), and use Freedom Unlimited for all non-category spending (1.5% beats the Flex's 1%). Both cost $0 annually. Together they're the best no-fee cash back pair available before adding a Sapphire card for transfers.

Bottom Line

The Chase Freedom Flex earns its place in virtually every Chase wallet. The $300/year in potential 5% category bonus, permanent 3% on dining, no foreign transaction fees, and gateway to transfer partners via Sapphire pairing are collectively unmatched at $0 annual fee. One clear weakness: the 1% base rate on non-category non-dining spending means it should always be paired with a flat-rate card like the Freedom Unlimited or Wells Fargo Active Cash. Apply for it if you're under Chase 5/24 — this card is worth one of those slots.
