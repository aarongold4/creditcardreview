The new Kroger credit card is worth it if most of your groceries come from Kroger pickup or delivery orders and you fill up at Kroger fuel centers — that's where its up-to-5X and 4X rates live. It is no longer a card for grocery shopping anywhere. The Kroger Rewards World Elite Mastercard was relaunched on September 30, 2026 by First Bank & Trust, with the fintech Imprint running the program, and the old U.S. Bank version's 5% mobile wallet rate did not make the trip. If you shop at more than one grocery chain, a general cash back card still beats it.

What Changed: The U.S. Bank Card Is Gone

For years, the Kroger card's big trick was 5% back on mobile wallet purchases at almost any merchant. That card belonged to U.S. Bank, and U.S. Bank's Kroger program has ended: existing cardholders were moved to the U.S. Bank Smartly Visa. The card that now carries the Kroger name is a different product from a different bank, with a points structure built around Kroger's own stores, app and fuel pumps.

If you held the old card, you don't automatically have the new one — it's a fresh application. Harris Teeter, part of the Kroger family, launched a matching card on October 30, 2026; see our Kroger vs. Harris Teeter comparison for how the two line up.

Read the "up to" carefully. The 5X on pickup and delivery and the 3X in store include points you would earn anyway: NerdWallet's breakdown shows the top rate stacks the card's own points with your regular Kroger loyalty points and Boost membership points. The card itself is adding less than the headline suggests. Outside Kroger, 2X on dining, utilities and travel and 1X on everything else is ordinary for a no-fee card.

There are no spending caps on the bonus categories, which is a real improvement over the old card's $3,000 mobile wallet limit.

The $100 Welcome Bonus — With Strings

New cardholders get a $100 reward after spending $750 outside Kroger channels within 90 days of opening the account. Two catches: the spending has to happen somewhere other than Kroger, and the $100 is redeemable only on eligible grocery purchases, not fuel (Upgraded Points). It's a modest offer — the Wells Fargo Active Cash pays $100 cash after $500 with no strings on how you spend it — but for a Kroger regular it's still found money.

Boost Membership, Birthday Reward and Fuel Points

The most tangible perk is a free Boost Essential membership, which Kroger normally sells for $69 a year. Add a $10 birthday reward each year and the card covers $79 in annual perks before you earn a point.

Points also work as Kroger fuel points: every 100 points takes 10¢ off per gallon at participating Kroger-family fuel centers, up to $1 per gallon on up to 35 gallons per fill-up. That's where the card is genuinely useful for drivers — combined with 4X at the pump, a regular Kroger fuel customer recycles card spending straight back into cheaper gas.

Points no longer expire on a fixed schedule; they last while the account is active and in good standing (an account with no transactions for 12 months is treated as inactive). The trade-off is that redemption is Kroger-only — groceries and fuel discounts, not cash in your bank account.

Check the Terms Before You Apply

Kroger's launch materials don't spell out the new card's APR range or its foreign transaction fee. Read the pricing terms on the application page before applying, and carry a balance on this card only if you have to — at typical store-card rates, interest wipes out any points you earn.

Bottom Line

The relaunched Kroger card is a narrower, more honest store card than the one it replaced. It's a good fit for one kind of household: Kroger pickup and delivery regulars who also buy their gas at Kroger and value a free Boost membership. Everyone else lost the reason to carry it when the 5% mobile wallet rate disappeared — a flat 2% card like the Citi Double Cash or Wells Fargo Active Cash, or an Amex Blue Cash card for supermarkets in general, will do more with the same grocery budget.
