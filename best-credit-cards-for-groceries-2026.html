Best Credit Cards for Groceries of 2026: Earn Up to 6% Back at the Supermarket

The average American household spends $519/month on groceries. The right credit card turns that into $200–$400 in annual rewards with zero extra effort. We compared every major grocery card by earnings rate, annual fee, and real-world take-home value.

📅 Updated April 2026
✍️ By Nathan Blake
⏱ 11 min read
Fact-checked

Groceries are one of the most consistent and predictable spending categories in any household budget — which makes them one of the best opportunities to earn credit card rewards. Unlike travel or dining, grocery spending happens every week regardless of your lifestyle. The right card silently earns you $200–$400 per year on purchases you'd make anyway.

But the grocery card category is also one of the most nuanced. Earn rates range from 2% to 6%, spending caps vary widely, and critical exclusions — Walmart, Target, Costco, and Sam's Club are excluded from the "supermarket" category on most cards — can mean the card that looks best on paper earns nothing at the stores you actually use.

1. Blue Cash Preferred® Card from American Express — Best Overall Grocery Card

The Blue Cash Preferred is the undisputed top grocery card in America and has been for years. Its 6% cash back at U.S. supermarkets is the highest flat rate available on any general-purpose card — and at the average U.S. household grocery spend of $519/month, you'll earn roughly $360 per year on groceries alone. The $95 annual fee (waived year one) is covered nearly four times over by grocery rewards at that spending level. Motley Fool Money named it Best Card for Groceries of 2026.

2. American Express Gold Card — Best for Groceries + Dining + Travel

For households spending heavily on both groceries and dining out, the Amex Gold is the superior card — even at $325/year. Its 4x Membership Rewards points at U.S. supermarkets (up to $25,000/yr) and 4x at restaurants worldwide are worth 4–8 cents per dollar when transferred to airline partners, beating the Blue Cash Preferred's 6 cents in pure cash when you're redeeming for premium travel. The $424 in annual credits (Uber Cash, dining, Resy, Dunkin') effectively reduce the net fee to under $0 for users who engage with all of them.

The Most Important Rule: Check the Exclusions

Every grocery card on this list excludes certain stores from earning bonus rewards. These exclusions can completely change which card is right for you — especially if your primary grocery shopping happens at stores that don't qualify as "supermarkets."

Store Qualifies as "Supermarket"? Notes
Kroger, Publix, Safeway, Albertsons ✓ Yes Traditional supermarkets — qualify on all cards
Trader Joe's, Whole Foods, Aldi ✓ Yes Qualify as supermarkets on most cards
Walmart, Target ✗ No Coded as superstores — excluded on all grocery cards
Costco, Sam's Club, BJ's ✗ No Wholesale clubs — excluded on all standard grocery cards
Instacart, Shipt (delivery) ⚠ Varies Counts for Chase Sapphire (online grocery category); excluded on Amex cards

If You Primarily Shop at Walmart, Target, or Costco

None of the cards on this list will earn you bonus rewards at those stores in the grocery category. For Walmart and Target shoppers, a flat-rate 2% card like the Wells Fargo Active Cash — which earns 2% on all purchases — is likely a better choice than a grocery-specific card that won't apply to your actual shopping habits.

The Bottom Line

For the majority of American households that spend $400–$600/month at traditional supermarkets, the Blue Cash Preferred wins decisively. At 6% cash back with a $95 annual fee that's waived year one, it delivers $300–$400+ in net annual value from grocery spending alone — more than any other card in this category.

If you also spend heavily on dining and plan to redeem for travel, the Amex Gold Card outperforms the Blue Cash Preferred significantly — its 4x points at U.S. supermarkets and restaurants worldwide are worth 8%+ when transferred to airline partners.

For households spending under $300/month on groceries, the Capital One SavorOne delivers 3% with zero annual fee and no math required.
