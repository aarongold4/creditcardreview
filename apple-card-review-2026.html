The Apple Card is Apple's co-branded Mastercard, issued by Goldman Sachs with Chase confirmed as the successor issuer (transition expected around 2028). It earns Daily Cash — cash back deposited to your Wallet app the same day a purchase posts — at tiered rates. For iPhone users who pay primarily with Apple Pay, the 2% on all Apple Pay transactions plus 3% at Apple and select partner merchants creates a compelling no-fee everyday card. The zero-fee structure (no annual, no late, no foreign transaction fees) is genuine and industry-leading.

Daily Cash posts to your Wallet app the same day a purchase clears — not at statement close, not monthly. This is the fastest cash back cycle available on any mainstream credit card.

What You Can Do With Daily Cash

  • Apple Pay purchases: Spend directly anywhere Apple Pay is accepted — Daily Cash functions as a spendable balance in Wallet
  • iMessage transfers: Send Daily Cash to any iPhone contact via Apple Messages
  • Apple Savings account: Route all Daily Cash automatically to a Goldman Sachs high-yield savings account (~3.65% APY as of late 2025) — earns interest passively
  • Bank transfer: Move to any linked bank account at any time, no minimum
  • Statement credit: Apply toward your Apple Card balance

Apple Card Monthly Installments — 0% on Apple Products

When buying eligible Apple hardware (iPhone, Mac, iPad, Apple Watch, AirPods, Apple TV) through Apple, you can select ACMI at checkout: 0% APR financing over 6, 12, or 24 months depending on the product. You still earn 3% Daily Cash upfront on the full purchase price. This makes the Apple Card the best way to finance Apple hardware — combining interest-free payments with 3% cash back.

Zero Fee Structure

Apple Card charges no fees of any kind: no annual fee, no late payment fee, no over-limit fee, no foreign transaction fee, no returned payment fee. Missing a payment does not trigger a penalty — but it does accrue additional interest at the card's standard APR (17.49–27.74% variable). The no-late-fee policy is unusually consumer-friendly, but responsible payment in full each month remains essential to avoid expensive interest charges.

Goldman Sachs → Chase (Expected ~2028)

In January 2026, Chase Bank (JPMorgan Chase) confirmed it will take over as Apple Card's issuing bank from Goldman Sachs. The transition is expected to complete around 2028 and will not require cardholders to re-apply. Changes to the rewards structure, billing cycle (currently end-of-month), and app experience have not been confirmed but are possible. Existing cardholders will receive advance notice of any material changes before they take effect.

Pros & Cons

What We Like

  • Daily Cash deposited same day — fastest rewards cycle on any mainstream card
  • Zero fees of any kind — annual, late, over-limit, and foreign all $0
  • 3% at Apple + select partners via Apple Pay
  • 0% ACMI financing on Apple hardware + 3% Daily Cash still earned
  • Apple Savings account earns ~3.65% APY on accumulated Daily Cash
  • Titanium physical card with no visible numbers — enhanced security
  • No foreign transaction fee — usable internationally at full value

What to Watch Out For

  • iPhone/iOS required — not available to Android users
  • 1% on physical card swipes — well below 1.5–2% flat-rate competitors
  • 2% Apple Pay base trails Wells Fargo Active Cash (2% everywhere, no Apple Pay required)
  • No welcome bonus — first-year value lower than most cash back cards
  • No balance transfers, no intro 0% APR (except ACMI)
  • No transfer partners, no travel redemptions — cash only
  • Issuer transition to Chase ~2028 may bring changes

Bottom Line

The Apple Card is the right card for iPhone users who pay with Apple Pay for most purchases and buy Apple products regularly. Same-day Daily Cash, zero fees of any kind, and 0% ACMI financing on Apple hardware are genuinely excellent features. The limitations are meaningful: Android users cannot apply, physical card swipes earn just 1%, and there is no welcome bonus. For heavy Apple Pay users, this card competes well against any 2% flat-rate card while adding 3% at Apple. Pair it with a general 2% flat-rate card for in-person spending where Apple Pay isn't accepted.
