Best Flat-Rate Cash Back Credit Cards of 2026: Every 2% (and 1.5%) Card Compared

No categories, no activation, no caps. Here is which flat-rate card actually pays the most — and the change this fall that reshuffled the 1.5% tier.

📅 Updated November 22, 2026
✍️ By Nathan Blake
⏱ 8 min read
Fact-checked

The best 2% cash back card for most people in late 2026 is the Citi Double Cash® if you want the bigger welcome bonus or an 18-month balance transfer window, and the Wells Fargo Active Cash® if you want an easier bonus, cell phone protection and a 0% APR on new purchases. Both pay 2% on everything with no annual fee. The Double Cash pays $200 after $1,500 in purchases in the first 6 months; the Active Cash pays $100 after $500 in 3 months. If you keep investments at Fidelity, the Fidelity® Rewards Visa Signature® also pays 2%, and Bank of America Rewards members at the top tiers can beat 2% with the Unlimited Cash Rewards.

Flat-rate cards are the "set it and forget it" layer of a wallet. They won't win any single category, but they win the purchases that don't fit anywhere else — insurance premiums, medical bills, home repairs, the hardware store, the vet. For many households that is a big slice of card spending, which is why the gap between 1.5% and 2% matters more than it looks.

What Changed This Fall

BofA Unlimited Cash Rewards: the first-year 2%-on-everything offer has ended; new cardholders earn 1.5%, with a $250 bonus after $1,000 in 90 days.

1. Citi Double Cash® Card — Best 2% Card for the Welcome Bonus and Balance Transfers

The Double Cash has always matched the Active Cash on earning — 1% when you buy and 1% when you pay, with no caps. Its bonus is the bigger of the two — $200 after $1,500 in purchases in the first 6 months — but it asks for three times the Active Cash's $500 of spend. If $1,500 over six months fits your normal spending, the Double Cash pays $100 more in year one.

It is also the better balance transfer card of the two: 0% on balance transfers for 18 months, with a 3% fee (minimum $5) on transfers made in the first 4 months, then 5%. There is no intro APR on purchases. Rewards post as ThankYou® Points, redeemable for cash back. The real drawback is the foreign transaction fee, which makes it the wrong card to carry abroad.

Best for: Anyone choosing a single 2% card today who can meet the $1,500 spend for the bigger bonus, or who needs an 18-month balance transfer window.

2. Wells Fargo Active Cash® Card — Best 2% Card for Extras

The Active Cash pays a straight 2% on every purchase with no "pay first" condition, plus $100 after $500 in purchases in the first 3 months. Where it beats the Double Cash is everything around the earn rate: a 0% intro APR for 12 months on both purchases and qualifying balance transfers, and cell phone protection of up to $600 per claim ($25 deductible, 2 claims per 12 months) when you pay your monthly phone bill with the card.

The bonus is smaller, but the $500 spend requirement is a third of the Double Cash's, and phone protection alone can be worth more than the $100 bonus gap after a single cracked screen. And a 0% window on new purchases is something the Double Cash simply doesn't offer. If you're financing a large purchase or want insurance on your phone, the Active Cash remains the better long-term card.

Best for: People who want the most complete no-fee 2% card, especially if they'll use the purchase APR or phone protection.

3. Fidelity® Rewards Visa Signature® — Best 2% Card for Investors

The Fidelity card earns 2% when rewards are deposited into an eligible Fidelity account — such as a brokerage account, IRA or HSA — with no annual fee and no foreign transaction fee. There is no welcome bonus, which is why it loses to the Double Cash and Active Cash in year one. But it is the only 2% card in this group that charges nothing abroad.

The catch is the routing: to get the full 2%, the money has to land in a Fidelity account. For anyone already saving there, that's a feature — every purchase becomes an automatic contribution.

Best for: Fidelity customers, and anyone who wants 2% with no foreign transaction fee.

4. Bank of America® Unlimited Cash Rewards — Best for BofA Rewards Members

On its own, this is a 1.5% card — and with the first-year 2% offer gone, it no longer matches the 2% cards out of the gate. The $250 bonus after $1,000 in 90 days and a 0% intro APR for 15 billing cycles keep it competitive in year one. Its real case is Bank of America Rewards: the boost lifts the rate to 1.65% for any BofA Rewards member, 1.875% at Preferred Plus ($30K in combined balances), 2.25% at Preferred Honors ($100K) and 2.625% at Premier ($1M) — the highest flat rate on any no-fee card here.

If you aren't at the $100K tier or above, a 2% card will out-earn it on everyday spending.

Best for: Bank of America and Merrill customers with $100K+ in qualifying balances.

5. Capital One Quicksilver — Best 1.5% Card for Travel and Intro APR

The Quicksilver earns 1.5% on everything plus 5% on hotels and rental cars booked through Capital One Travel, with $200 after $500 in purchases and no annual fee. Two things set it apart from the 2% cards: no foreign transaction fee, and a 0% intro APR for 15 months on purchases and balance transfers — three months longer than the Active Cash on purchases.

At 1.5%, it costs you $50 a year for every $10,000 of spending versus a 2% card. That's the price of the longer 0% window and fee-free use abroad.

Best for: A no-fee flat-rate card that also works overseas, or a 15-month 0% window on a big purchase.

Read the full Capital One Quicksilver review →

6. PayPal Cashback Mastercard® — Best if You Check Out With PayPal

Technically a hybrid: 3% when you pay with PayPal at checkout, 1.5% everywhere else, no annual fee and no bonus. For heavy online shoppers who already use PayPal, the 3% tier can push the blended rate above 2%. For everyone else it is a 1.5% card, and the 3% tier only applies at stores that offer PayPal at checkout.

Best for: Online shoppers who route most purchases through PayPal.

Business Owners: The Flat-Rate Option

None of the cards above are business cards. The flat-rate pick on that side is the Chase Ink Business Unlimited®: 1.5% on everything, $0 annual fee and a $750 bonus after $6,000 in 3 months — the largest bonus of any card on this page. The Bottom Line

Get the Citi Double Cash if you want the biggest bonus from a 2% card (and can spend $1,500 in 6 months) or need an 18-month balance transfer window. Get the Wells Fargo Active Cash if phone protection or a 0% APR on purchases matters to you — those perks outlast any bonus. Fidelity investors should take the Fidelity Rewards Visa, and BofA customers at Preferred Honors or above should look hard at the Unlimited Cash Rewards. Whichever you choose, pair it with a category card — see Best Cash Back Credit Cards of 2026 and Best No Annual Fee Cards.
