Chase Sapphire Preferred vs. Sapphire Reserve 2026: Which Sapphire Is Worth It After the Hyatt Change?

The $95 Preferred is the right Sapphire for most people. The $795 Reserve wins for heavy travelers, lounge users and Hyatt fans.

📅 Updated November 2, 2026
✍️ By Nathan Blake
⏳ 8 min read
✓ Fact-checked

The Chase Sapphire Preferred is the better Sapphire for most people: it costs $95 a year against the Reserve's $795, and it now earns 3x on gas, groceries bought online and streaming that the Reserve doesn't. The Sapphire Reserve is worth it if you will use most of its credits, want lounge access, or transfer a lot of points to World of Hyatt. Hyatt is the new dividing line. Since October 1, 2026, every Sapphire Preferred cardmember transfers to Hyatt at 4:3, while the Reserve still transfers at 1:1.

The Hyatt Change, and Why It Matters

Chase moved new Sapphire Preferred applicants to a 4:3 Hyatt transfer ratio on June 15, 2026, and moved existing Preferred accounts to the same ratio on October 1, 2026. Every 4,000 Chase points you send from a Preferred now becomes 3,000 Hyatt points. The Reserve kept 1:1.

That is a 25% haircut on what is, for many points collectors, the best use of Chase points. If you transfer 60,000 Chase points a year to Hyatt, a Preferred turns them into 45,000 Hyatt points; a Reserve turns them into 60,000. The Points Guy estimates that about 60,000 Hyatt points a year is the point where the Reserve's Hyatt advantage alone starts to justify it. That estimate depends on its own point valuation, so treat it as a rough guide.

All of Chase's other transfer partners are the same on both cards: 10 airlines (including United, Southwest, Air Canada Aeroplan, British Airways and Air France-KLM Flying Blue) and 3 other hotel programs (IHG, Marriott and Wyndham).

If You Never Transfer to Hyatt

The Hyatt change doesn't affect you. Compare the cards on credits, lounges and everyday earning instead.

Is the Reserve Worth $700 More?

The Reserve's $795 fee drops to an effective $495 once you use the $300 annual travel credit. That leaves a $400 gap over the Preferred. The rest has to come from credits you would spend anyway:

Count only the credits you would use without the card. A traveler who uses the $300 travel credit, one Edit stay a year and the dining credit has covered the fee gap with room to spare. A traveler who would have to book a hotel they didn't want or eat at a restaurant they wouldn't choose is paying $700 for coupons.

The Preferred has smaller perks of its own: the $100 Chase Travel hotel credit, a $120 Global Entry credit, DashPass with a $10 monthly DoorDash promo for non-restaurant orders, and a year of Apple TV if you activate by December 31, 2026.

Earning: Each Card Wins Different Categories

The Reserve earns more on travel: 8x through Chase Travel and 4x on flights and hotels booked directly. The Preferred earns 5x through Chase Travel and 2x on other travel.

The Preferred wins everyday spending. Its 3x on gas and EV charging, online groceries and streaming all earn just 1x on the Reserve. Both earn 3x on dining. For a household that spends more at the pump and on grocery delivery than on flights, the $95 card can out-earn the $795 one.

Points Boost also favors the Reserve. When you redeem through Chase Travel, select hotels and flights are worth up to 2 cents a point on the Reserve versus up to 1.5 cents on the Preferred.

What Happened to the Preferred's Anniversary Bonus?

The Preferred's 10% anniversary points bonus is gone. Cardmembers who applied from June 15, 2026 never get it. Older accounts earned it on purchases through October 1, 2026, and Chase says those last bonuses post by January 31, 2027. The Reserve never had one.

Lounges and Travel Insurance

Only the Reserve includes lounge access: Priority Pass, Chase's Sapphire Lounges by The Club with two free guests, and Air Canada Maple Leaf lounges. If you fly several times a year from airports with those lounges, this is often the single biggest reason to pay for the Reserve.

The insurance gap is narrower than the fee gap. Both cards have primary rental car coverage ($60,000 on the Preferred, $75,000 on the Reserve) and trip cancellation and interruption coverage up to $10,000 per traveler and $20,000 per trip. The Reserve's trip delay coverage starts at 6 hours instead of 12.

Can You Have Both Sapphire Cards?

Yes. Chase dropped its "one Sapphire" rule, and since January 2026 its bonus terms work per card. You can't open a card you already hold, and you may not get the bonus if you previously held that card or earned its bonus. Holding one Sapphire doesn't stop you from earning the other card's bonus. The 5/24 rule still applies. Upgrading or downgrading between the two doesn't earn a bonus, and you need to hold a card for 12 months before changing it.
